Visiting Philadelphia

A little help with getting here, getting around,
and what to do with the rest of your weekend.

Getting to the party

The party is at 1220 North Front Street, in Fishtown — a short ride north-east of Center City.

By train
The Front–Girard stop is right at the corner. It's on the L (the Market–Frankford Line, the blue one — you may still hear locals call it "the El"), about 10 minutes from Center City.
By trolley or bus
The Route 15 trolley runs along Girard Avenue, and buses 5 and 25 stop nearby.
By car
Street parking only, and Fishtown gets busy on a Friday night. Carpool, take transit, or use a rideshare if you can.
Remember
Please be inside by 7:15 PM. If you're going to be later than that, come after 8:00 PM, once the surprise is over.

Getting around & paying for it

Philadelphia's transit is run by SEPTA. The good news for visitors: you don't need to buy anything. Just tap a contactless credit or debit card — or Apple Pay, Google Pay or Samsung Pay on your phone — directly on the reader as you board.

What a ride costs
About $2.90 for a bus, subway or trolley ride, and your first two transfers within 90 minutes are free.
Traveling together
One contactless card or phone can pay for up to five people — tap once per rider.
From the airport (PHL)
The Airport Line regional rail train runs from every terminal into Center City in around 25 minutes. It's $5 if you tap or use a SEPTA Key card, $7 if you pay the conductor onboard. Under-12s ride free with an adult.
From 30th Street Station
If you're coming by Amtrak, you're already downtown. Hop on the L heading east and you're 15 minutes from Fishtown.
The two main lines
L (blue) runs east–west along Market Street. B (orange, the Broad Street Line) runs north–south. Almost everything you'll want is near one of them.

A SEPTA Key card exists too ($4.95, refunded if you register it), but for a weekend visit tapping your own card is simpler.

Neighborhoods worth your time

  • Fishtown & Northern Liberties — where the party is. Former industrial blocks turned into the city's best stretch of bars, music venues, coffee and pizza. Frankford Avenue is the spine.
  • Old City — the postcard Philadelphia. Independence Hall, the Liberty Bell, cobblestones and Elfreth's Alley, the oldest continuously inhabited street in the country. Walkable from Fishtown along the river.
  • Center City & Rittenhouse Square — downtown. Rittenhouse is the leafy square everyone sits in; the streets around it have the city's most polished restaurants and shopping.
  • South Philly & East Passyunk — the Italian Market, cheesesteak arguments, and along East Passyunk Avenue one of the best-value restaurant rows anywhere in the city.
  • University City — Penn and Drexel, across the river. Worth it for Clark Park and the food trucks.
  • Chinatown — right by the convention center, and open far later than most of the city.
  • The Parkway museums — the Art Museum steps (yes, those steps), the Barnes Foundation and the Rodin, all in a row.

A few practical things

  • The city is a grid, and a small one. Numbered streets run north–south, tree-named streets (Chestnut, Walnut, Spruce, Pine) run east–west. Broad Street is 14th. Most of Center City is walkable end to end in half an hour.
  • September evenings are usually mild but can turn cool once the sun goes down, and the venue has outdoor space — bring a layer.
  • It's BYOB at the party, and that's a Philadelphia institution: a lot of the city's best restaurants are BYOB too, so it's worth asking before you book.
  • Tipping is 18–20% at restaurants and bars, as elsewhere in the US.
